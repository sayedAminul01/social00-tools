What Is Loan EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount you pay a lender every month until a loan is fully repaid. Each EMI is a mix of two things: interest on the money you still owe, and a portion that actually reduces what you owe (the principal). Because the EMI itself stays constant, that mix changes every month as the loan progresses.

The EMI Formula, Explained

The standard formula is EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months. When the rate is 0%, this formula breaks down (division by zero), so a 0% loan simply uses EMI = P ÷ n instead.

Why Interest Dominates Early Payments

Interest for any given month is calculated on the balance still outstanding at that point — and early in a loan, that balance is close to the full principal, so the interest slice of your EMI is largest. As you pay down the balance, the interest owed each month shrinks, and since the EMI is fixed, the leftover — the principal slice — grows. Late in the loan, your balance is small, so almost the whole EMI goes toward principal. This pattern is called amortization.

How Rate, Tenure & Prepayment Affect Total Interest

A higher annual rate increases the interest charged on every month's balance, raising both your EMI and total interest. A longer tenure lowers your monthly EMI but keeps a balance outstanding for more months, so total interest paid actually goes up. Prepaying extra toward principal shrinks the balance immediately, which reduces the interest that accrues on it for every remaining month — even modest, early prepayments can meaningfully cut the total interest bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI and how is it calculated?

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is the fixed amount you pay each month toward a loan. It's calculated as EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the tenure in months.

Why do I pay more interest in the early months?

Interest each month is charged on the remaining balance, which is largest at the start of the loan. As the balance shrinks with each payment, the interest portion shrinks too, so a growing share of your fixed EMI goes toward principal over time.

What happens if my loan has 0% interest?

The standard EMI formula divides by zero when the rate is 0%, so this calculator handles it as a special case: EMI simply equals the principal divided by the number of months, with no interest component at all.

Does prepaying my loan reduce total interest?

Yes. Any extra payment reduces the outstanding balance immediately, and since interest is calculated on that balance each month, a lower balance means less interest accrues for the rest of the loan — even a small prepayment early on can meaningfully cut total interest.

Does a longer tenure always cost more overall?

For the same principal and rate, a longer tenure lowers your monthly EMI but increases total interest paid, because the balance stays higher for longer and accrues interest over more months. A shorter tenure raises the EMI but reduces total interest.

Is this calculator's result the exact amount my bank will charge?

No — this is an educational estimate using the standard EMI formula. Real loans can include processing fees, insurance, variable or reset interest rates, and different compounding conventions that change the actual figure. Always check your lender's official amortization schedule for the real number.
