Why Resizing Matters for Social & Page Speed

Every platform crops, compresses, or rejects images that don't match its expected dimensions — an oversized upload gets squashed by the platform's own compressor, often looking worse than if you'd resized it properly yourself. On your own site, an unresized image forces visitors to download pixels they never see, slowing page load and hurting Core Web Vitals and SEO. Resizing to the exact dimension you need keeps quality under your control and pages fast.

Aspect Ratio, Explained Simply

Aspect ratio is the relationship between width and height — 1080×1080 is a 1:1 square, while 1080×1350 is a 4:5 portrait rectangle. Force a square photo into 1080×1350 without locking the ratio and it visibly stretches taller; crop or letterbox it instead and nothing distorts. That's why this tool locks the ratio by default — type a width and the matching height is computed automatically from your original image.

Lossless (PNG) vs Lossy (JPEG) Resizing

PNG re-encodes every pixel exactly, so resizing never adds compression artifacts — ideal for logos, screenshots, and graphics with sharp edges or transparency, at the cost of a larger file. JPEG uses lossy compression tuned by a quality setting (this tool defaults to 0.92, a near-invisible loss); it produces much smaller files and is the standard choice for photos.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will resizing distort my image?

Only if "Lock aspect ratio" is off and your new width/height don't match the original image's ratio — the image will stretch to fill those exact dimensions. Keep the lock checkbox on to resize proportionally with no distortion.

What image size should I use for Instagram, YouTube or Twitter?

Use the dimension cheat-sheet above, or click a preset button in the tool — Instagram Post (1080×1080), Instagram Story (1080×1920), YouTube Thumbnail (1280×720), Twitter/X Post (1600×900) and Profile Picture (500×500) are filled in automatically.

PNG vs JPEG output — which should I pick?

Pick PNG for logos, graphics, screenshots or anything needing transparency — it's lossless. Pick JPEG for photos; it compresses much smaller with a quality loss that's barely visible at 0.92 quality.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. This tool uses the browser's Canvas API to resize your image locally on your device. Nothing is ever sent to a server.

Can I resize up (enlarge) an image?

Yes, technically you can set a width/height larger than the original. But enlarging doesn't create new detail, so the result will look softer or blurrier than a photo actually taken or exported at that size.

Is this tool free?

Yes — completely free, no account, no watermark, and no limit on how many images you resize.
