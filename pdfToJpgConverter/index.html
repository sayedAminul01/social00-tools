Why Convert a PDF Page to JPG?

PDFs are great for documents, but a JPG image is often what you actually need — dropping a single page into a slide deck, pasting it into a chat or email, using it as a thumbnail preview, or sharing one page on social media without sending the whole file. Converting to JPG turns a PDF page into something every app, platform and person can open instantly, no PDF viewer required.

How This Converter Works

This tool uses PDF.js, Mozilla's open-source PDF rendering engine, loaded directly in your browser. Each page is drawn onto an off-screen <canvas> element at your chosen scale, then the canvas is encoded to a JPG using the browser's built-in canvas.toBlob() API. No PDF parsing or rendering happens on a server — the entire pipeline runs on your own device.

Render Quality: Standard vs High

Standard (1.5x scale) renders each page at a moderate resolution — fast to process and reasonably sized files, good for sharing or on-screen previews. High (2.5x scale) renders roughly 2.5 times the pixel density, producing noticeably sharper images better suited for printing or zooming in on fine text and detail, at the cost of larger file sizes and longer processing time per page.

Scanned PDFs vs Text-Based PDFs

It doesn't matter whether your PDF was exported from a word processor or is a scanned photo of a paper document — this tool renders each page visually, the same way a PDF viewer displays it on screen, rather than extracting the underlying text. That means scanned pages, vector graphics, embedded fonts and photos all convert identically and reliably.

Common Use Cases

  • Sharing a single page from a long report without sending the whole PDF
  • Embedding a PDF page as an image in a slide deck or document
  • Generating quick thumbnail previews of a PDF's contents
  • Posting a page to social media, where PDFs usually aren't supported

Why This Runs Entirely in Your Browser

Because rendering happens locally via PDF.js and the Canvas API, your document never has to be uploaded anywhere — which matters for contracts, IDs, resumes and anything else you'd rather not send to a third-party server. It's also free, unlimited, and works the moment the page has loaded.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. This tool runs entirely in your browser using PDF.js, Mozilla's open-source PDF rendering library. Your PDF is read locally, rendered locally, and never leaves your device or touches any server.

What's the difference between Standard and High render quality?

Standard renders each page at 1.5x scale — smaller files, faster processing, good for sharing or previews. High renders at 2.5x scale, producing sharper, larger images better suited for printing or zooming in on fine detail. Higher scale takes longer and produces bigger JPG files.

Will scanned PDFs work, or only text-based ones?

Both work identically. This tool renders each page visually — like taking a screenshot of it — rather than extracting text, so a scanned photo of a document and a text-based PDF convert exactly the same way.

Can I download just one page instead of all of them?

Yes. Every page gets its own thumbnail and its own individual download link, so you can save exactly the pages you need. The "Download All" button is just a convenience that triggers each page's download in sequence.

Is there a limit on PDF size or page count?

There's no hard limit set by this tool, but very large or very long PDFs take longer to render and use more of your device's memory since everything happens on your own hardware. Most everyday PDFs — reports, slides, scans — convert in a few seconds per page.

Is this tool free?

Yes — completely free, no account required, no watermark, and no limit on how many PDFs you convert. Everything runs locally in your browser.
