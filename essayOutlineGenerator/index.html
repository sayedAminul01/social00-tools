What is an Essay Outline?

An essay outline is a structured plan that organizes your ideas before you start writing. It serves as a roadmap for your essay, helping you arrange your thoughts logically and ensuring your arguments flow coherently from introduction to conclusion.

A well-crafted outline includes your thesis statement, main arguments, supporting evidence, and a clear structure that guides readers through your reasoning.

How to Write an Essay Outline

  1. Choose your topic: Select a clear, focused topic that you can adequately cover in your essay length
  2. Develop your thesis: Create a strong thesis statement that presents your main argument or position
  3. Identify main points: List 3-5 main arguments or ideas that support your thesis
  4. Add supporting details: For each main point, include evidence, examples, or analysis
  5. Plan introduction: Outline your hook, background information, and thesis placement
  6. Structure body paragraphs: Organize each paragraph with topic sentence, evidence, and analysis
  7. Draft conclusion: Plan how you'll restate your thesis and summarize key points

Essay Structure Tips

Introduction (10% of essay): Start with an attention-grabbing hook, provide background context, and end with a clear thesis statement that previews your main arguments.

Body Paragraphs (80% of essay): Each paragraph should focus on one main idea. Start with a topic sentence, provide evidence or examples, analyze the evidence, and connect back to your thesis.

Conclusion (10% of essay): Restate your thesis in new words, summarize your main arguments, and provide closing thoughts or call to action. Never introduce new information in the conclusion.

How to Write a Strong Thesis Statement

A thesis statement is the backbone of your essay. It should be:

  • Specific: Clearly state your position without being vague
  • Arguable: Present a claim that others might disagree with
  • Focused: Cover only what you can adequately discuss in your essay
  • Clear: Use straightforward language that readers can understand
  • Positioned correctly: Usually appears at the end of your introduction

Example: "Social media has negatively impacted mental health, interpersonal relationships, and productivity among young adults, requiring urgent policy interventions."
