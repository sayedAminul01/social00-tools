    What Is This JPG to PDF Converter?

    This tool combines one or more JPG or PNG images into a single, real, downloadable PDF file — built byte by byte directly in your browser with no external library and no server upload. Add your images, put them in the order you want, and generate a PDF you can preview and download immediately.

    Why Combine Images Into a Single PDF?

    A folder of loose image files is awkward to share and easy to lose track of. A single PDF is the standard, expected format for scanned documents, photo or design portfolios, and file submissions to schools, employers, or government forms — recipients can open one file, in the right order, on any device.

    How the Conversion Works

    Every image is drawn onto an offscreen <canvas> and re-exported as a JPEG. Those JPEG bytes are then embedded directly inside a PDF Image XObject using the standard /DCTDecode filter — the raw compressed image data is wrapped in valid PDF structure, not re-parsed or converted through any third-party library.

    Choosing a Page Size

    Original makes each PDF page exactly match that image's own pixel dimensions, so the image fills the page edge to edge with no border — ideal for portfolios or scans. A4 and Letter place every image, scaled to fit and centered with a small margin, onto a standard printable page — ideal if you plan to print the result.

    Image Quality & JPEG Re-encoding

    Every image — including files that are already JPEGs — is re-encoded at 92% JPEG quality when it's drawn to canvas, and PNG transparency is flattened onto a white background. This keeps the process simple and reliable across formats. At 92% quality the loss is minor and rarely visible, but it is a real, honest tradeoff worth knowing about.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Why combine JPG images into a PDF?

    A single PDF is far easier to share, print, and store than a folder of loose image files. It's the standard format for scanned documents, photo portfolios, and file submissions to schools, employers, or government forms — one file instead of many attachments.

    Will converting to PDF reduce my image quality?

    There is a small quality tradeoff. Every image is re-encoded as a JPEG at 92% quality before being embedded, so already-compressed JPEGs may lose a touch of detail on re-encoding, and PNGs lose transparency (converted to a white background). At 92% quality the difference is minor and rarely visible.

    Which page size should I choose — Original, A4, or Letter?

    Original makes each PDF page exactly match that image's own pixel dimensions, with no borders — best for portfolios or scans you want reproduced edge to edge. A4 and Letter fit every image, centered with a small margin, onto a standard printable page size — best if the PDF will be printed.

    Can I combine PNG or other image types too?

    Yes. Any image file you drop in — PNG, WebP, GIF, and more — is automatically converted to JPEG internally before being embedded in the PDF, so you can mix formats freely in one file.

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. Everything happens locally in your browser using the Canvas API to build the PDF byte by byte. Your images are never uploaded anywhere, and non-JPEG images are converted to JPEG internally as part of building the file, entirely on your device.

    Can I reorder or remove images before converting?

    Yes. Each thumbnail has up and down buttons to change its page order, plus a remove button to drop it entirely. You can also add more images at any point before generating the PDF.
