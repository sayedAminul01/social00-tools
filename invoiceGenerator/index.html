What Should a Professional Invoice Include?

A complete invoice needs a unique invoice number, the issue date and due date, your business details and the client's details, an itemized breakdown of the work or goods, the total amount due, and clear payment terms. Leaving any of these out is one of the most common reasons invoices get questioned, delayed, or sent back for corrections.

Why Itemizing Every Charge Builds Trust

A single lump-sum total gives a client nothing to check it against. Breaking charges into individual lines — what was delivered, how many units, at what rate — lets them verify the invoice against what was agreed. If a question ever comes up, both sides can point to the exact line instead of re-litigating the whole bill.

Tax Before or After Discount? It Depends Where You Are

This tool applies the discount to the subtotal first, then calculates tax on the discounted amount — a common convention, but not a universal one. Some tax authorities require tax on the pre-discount subtotal instead. Treat the order used here as a sensible default, not tax advice, and confirm the correct sequence with your local tax rules before relying on it.

Invoice Numbering Is a Small Habit With a Big Payoff

A unique, sequential invoice number is the reference both you and your client use to match a payment to a job in bookkeeping software and tax filings. This tool suggests one based on today's date, but you can edit it to fit whatever numbering scheme your accounting already uses.

Payment Terms That Actually Get You Paid Faster

A specific due date and a stated payment method beat a vague "payment due soon" every time — specific instructions are far less likely to slip down a client's to-do list. Note your accepted payment methods and any late-payment policy directly in the invoice's notes section.

How This Generator Works — and What It Doesn't Do

Everything you type is calculated and rendered locally in your browser; nothing is uploaded to a server. The "Print / Save as PDF" button uses your browser's own native print engine — the same preview you see on screen is what gets printed, so there's no separate server-side PDF renderer and no risk of the numbers drifting between what you see and what you print.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a professional invoice include?

At minimum: a unique invoice number, the issue date and due date, your business details and the client's details, an itemized list of charges, the total due, and clear payment terms. Missing any of these is a common reason invoices get questioned or paid late.

Why itemize charges instead of billing one lump sum?

A single total gives a client nothing to check it against. Itemized lines let them verify quantities and rates against what was agreed, which builds trust and makes any dispute quick to resolve by pointing at the exact line in question.

Should tax be calculated before or after the discount?

Conventions differ by country and business. This tool applies the discount to the subtotal first, then calculates tax on the discounted amount — a common approach, but not universal. Always follow your local tax authority's rules rather than assuming this order is correct for your situation.

Why does every invoice need a unique invoice number?

It's the reference both sides use for record-keeping, bookkeeping software, and tax filing. Without one, matching a payment back to a specific job becomes guesswork for you and your client's accounts team.

What payment terms should I put on an invoice?

State the due date explicitly, the accepted payment methods (bank transfer, card, etc.), and any late-payment policy. Vague terms like "payment due soon" are far more likely to slip to the bottom of a client's to-do list than a specific date.

Is my invoice data uploaded anywhere, and how does the PDF export work?

No — everything you type stays in your browser and nothing is sent to any server. The "Print / Save as PDF" button calls your browser's native print function; in the dialog that opens, choose "Save as PDF" (or "Microsoft Print to PDF" on Windows) as the destination to produce a real PDF file. This isn't a server-side generator, just your browser's own printing engine.
