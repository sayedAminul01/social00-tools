    What Is an XML Sitemap?

    An XML sitemap is a file that lists the URLs on your site so search engines can find them more easily. Each entry can carry a last-modified date, a change-frequency hint, and a relative priority. It's a discovery aid for crawlers, not a piece of content that visitors ever see.

    Why Sitemaps Help — And What They Don't Do

    The real benefit of a sitemap is helping search engines discover pages that have weak internal linking — new posts, deep archive pages, or pages buried behind JavaScript navigation. It is not a ranking-boost mechanism: Google still evaluates content quality, links, and relevance completely independently of whether a URL appears in your sitemap.

    The 50,000-URL / 50MB Limit

    Per the official sitemaps.org protocol, a single sitemap file cannot list more than 50,000 URLs and cannot exceed 50MB uncompressed. Sites larger than that split their URLs across multiple sitemap files and list those files inside a separate sitemap index file, which this tool will warn you about but does not generate for you.

    Changefreq and Priority Are Hints, Not Commands

    Google has said publicly that it treats <changefreq> and <priority> as hints it may largely ignore, since it decides for itself how often to recrawl a page and how that page compares to the rest of your site. Set them if you like for completeness — just don't expect them to force a particular crawl schedule.

    Where to Place and Submit Your Sitemap

    Convention is to place sitemap.xml at the root of your domain, e.g. example.com/sitemap.xml. From there, submit it directly in Google Search Console, or add a Sitemap: line pointing to it inside your robots.txt file so any crawler can find it automatically.

    How This Generator Works

    Each pasted line is trimmed, combined with your base domain unless it's already a full URL, and validated with the browser's own URL parser. Exact duplicates are removed, invalid lines are skipped and listed rather than silently breaking the file, and special characters like & in query strings are properly escaped to &amp; so the resulting XML always parses cleanly.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is an XML sitemap for?

    An XML sitemap lists your site's URLs so search engines can discover pages, especially ones with weak internal linking. It doesn't directly boost rankings — it just helps crawlers find and understand your content faster.

    Do changefreq and priority affect my ranking?

    Not directly. Google has stated it treats <changefreq> and <priority> as hints it may largely ignore, since it independently determines how often to recrawl a page and how important that page is.

    How many URLs can one sitemap file hold?

    Per the sitemaps.org protocol, a single sitemap file is limited to 50,000 URLs and 50MB uncompressed. Larger sites split their URLs across multiple sitemap files and list those files in a sitemap index file.

    Where do I put my sitemap and how do I submit it?

    Upload sitemap.xml to your site's root, e.g. example.com/sitemap.xml, then submit it in Google Search Console or reference it with a Sitemap: line in your robots.txt file.

    Does this tool send my URLs anywhere?

    No. Parsing, validation and XML generation all run locally in your browser. Your URL list is never uploaded or transmitted anywhere.
