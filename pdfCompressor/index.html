What Is This PDF Compressor?

This tool shrinks a PDF's file size by finding JPEG images embedded inside it and re-encoding them at a lower quality — the same technique most desktop and server-side PDF compressors use, just running locally instead of on someone else's server. It uses PDF.js and pdf-lib, both loaded from your browser, so nothing about your document ever leaves your device.

How This Tool Compresses Your PDF

Each page is scanned for image objects that use the standard DCTDecode (JPEG) filter. Every matching image is decoded, optionally shrunk to a maximum of 1600 pixels on its longer side, and re-saved as a JPEG at your chosen quality. If the new version isn't actually smaller than the original, that specific image is left untouched — so compression never makes an individual image worse.

What This Tool Won't Shrink (By Design)

This is a deliberately narrow, safety-first tool. It never touches fonts, text, or vector graphics, and it skips any image that isn't an unambiguous, single-filter JPEG — that includes PNG-style images, CMYK or indexed color, images with transparency masks, and anything else that can't be round-tripped with full confidence. A text-only or vector-only PDF has nothing this tool can compress, and it will tell you that honestly rather than pretending to shrink it.

Choosing a Compression Level

Low compression keeps images at their original resolution and re-encodes them at ~80% JPEG quality — a light touch for when quality matters most. Recommended (the default) uses ~60% quality and caps images at 1600px on the longer side, which is plenty for on-screen viewing and most printing. High compression pushes to ~40% quality with the same 1600px cap, for the smallest possible file at some visible quality cost.

Privacy: Everything Happens in Your Browser

  • Your PDF is read locally and never uploaded to a server
  • Image decoding, re-encoding, and the final export all run on your device
  • Nothing is stored, logged, or sent anywhere — close the tab and it's gone
  • Free, unlimited, no sign-up

Why We Chose a Conservative Approach

A PDF's internal structure can hide a lot of complexity — different color spaces, transparency masks, unusual encodings — and getting any of it wrong can silently corrupt a file. Rather than attempt to handle every possible case, this tool only acts when it's completely certain an image can be safely re-encoded, and leaves everything else exactly as it was. A narrower tool that never breaks your file beats a broader one that sometimes does.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my PDF uploaded to a server?

No. This tool loads and compresses your PDF entirely in your browser using PDF.js and pdf-lib. Your file is never uploaded to any server — everything, including the final download, happens on your own device.

What does this tool actually compress?

It re-encodes JPEG images embedded inside your PDF at a lower quality and, at the Recommended and High settings, shrinks any image wider or taller than 1600 pixels. It does not touch fonts, text, vector graphics, or the PDF's page structure.

Why didn't my PDF get smaller?

Text-only or vector-only PDFs have no embedded photos for this tool to shrink, and some PDFs already use tightly compressed or non-JPEG images with nothing safe left to gain. In both cases this tool tells you honestly instead of faking a result.

Can this corrupt or damage my PDF?

This tool is deliberately conservative: only images using the DCTDecode (JPEG) filter are touched, and only when the re-encoded version is actually smaller than the original. Anything else — PNG-style images, CMYK or indexed color, transparency masks, and similar — is left completely untouched rather than risk a broken result.

Can this tool open password-protected PDFs?

No. Encrypted or password-protected PDFs can't be opened by the PDF.js and pdf-lib libraries this tool runs in your browser, so you'll need to remove the password with another tool first.

Is this tool free?

Yes — completely free, no account required, no file limit imposed by this tool, and no watermark. Everything runs locally in your browser.
