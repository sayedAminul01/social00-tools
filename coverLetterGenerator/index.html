Ideal Length: Under 400 Words

A cover letter should fit comfortably on a single page — roughly 250-400 words across four short paragraphs. Anything longer tends to get skimmed rather than read, which defeats the purpose of a letter meant to earn a closer look at your resume.

What Recruiters Actually Skim For

In the first few seconds, most recruiters look for the exact job title, the company name, and two or three concrete skills or results — not a life story. Put those where they're easy to spot, ideally in the opening paragraph, or the letter risks being skipped.

Common Cover Letter Mistakes

The biggest offenders: a generic opening that could apply to any company, simply restating the resume line by line instead of adding context, no evidence you researched the role, and a closing that doesn't clearly ask for an interview or next step.

The Four-Paragraph Structure

This generator builds a letter in four parts: an opening that names the role and company, a fit paragraph that weaves in your skills and experience, a value paragraph that states what you bring, and a closing call-to-action that ends with your name.

How to Customize the Output

Before sending, add one sentence about something specific to the company (a product, a value, recent news), replace a generic claim with a real number from your work history, and read the whole letter aloud once — if it doesn't sound like you, adjust the wording.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a cover letter be?

Keep it under 400 words and to a single page. Hiring managers skim, so four tight paragraphs that get to the point beat a long letter that repeats your resume.

What do recruiters actually skim for?

The job title, the company name, and two or three concrete skills or results in the first few lines. If those aren't easy to spot in the opening paragraph, most recruiters move on before reaching paragraph two.

What are the most common cover letter mistakes?

A generic opening that could apply to any job, simply restating the resume line by line instead of adding context, no mention of the specific company or role, and a closing that doesn't ask for next steps.

Should I customize the generated letter before sending it?

Yes, always. Treat the output as a strong first draft: add a specific detail about the company, swap in a real achievement with a number where you can, and read it aloud once to make sure it sounds like you.

Is this powered by a live AI model?
