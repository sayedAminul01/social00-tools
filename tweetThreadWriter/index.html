The Real 280-Character Limit

A standard tweet on X is capped at 280 characters — every letter, space, punctuation mark and emoji counts (most emoji count as 2 characters). Links are the one exception: X auto-shortens any URL to a fixed length regardless of how long the original is. This tool counts your generated text live so you can see exactly where you stand before you paste it in, with the counter shifting from green to amber to red as you approach and cross the limit.

Why Threads Outperform a Single Tweet

A single tweet has to land its whole point in one breath. A thread instead spreads one idea across several tweets — one beat per reply — which keeps each tap-to-expand feeling worth it, holds attention longer, and gives the platform more engagement signals from a single piece of writing (replies, dwell time, profile visits) than one isolated post ever generates. For anything with real depth — a lesson, a breakdown, a story — a thread almost always earns more reach than trying to cram it into 280 characters.

What Makes a Good Hook Tweet

The first tweet in a thread has one job: earn the tap. A strong hook promises a specific, concrete payoff ("3 things nobody tells you about X") rather than just announcing a topic ("a thread about X"). Ending the hook with a thread emoji (🧵) or a "(1/N)" counter is a small but real signal to readers — and to X's own UI — that more content follows, which measurably increases how often people expand the full thread.

Choosing the Right Thread Length

There's no universal right answer, but 5-7 tweets is a reliable sweet spot for most topics — enough room to develop one idea properly without losing readers halfway through. A 10-tweet thread works better for genuinely dense material, like a full step-by-step breakdown, where every single tweet is pulling real weight. If a beat feels like filler, the thread is too long, no matter what the target length was.
